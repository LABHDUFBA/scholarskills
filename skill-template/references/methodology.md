# Methodological grounding

## 1. Method implemented

Name the method and the tradition it comes from. Cite literature that can be checked — author,
work, year, and the specific claim being taken from it. If a citation cannot be verified, remove
it; a wrong citation is worse than no citation.

## 2. Operationalisation

Which concepts became which variables, and which decisions that entailed. Every choice that could
plausibly have gone the other way belongs here, with the alternative and the reason.

## 3. Evidence versus hypothesis

State separately:

- what the method *establishes* when its assumptions hold;
- what remains an *interpretation*;
- what is a *hypothesis* the skill does not test;
- what is a *normative* position, if any.

## 4. Assumptions and validity domain

List the assumptions and, for each, what breaks when it is violated. State the domain in which the
output can be read as valid.

## 5. Known alternatives

At least one competing approach, and why it was not chosen. A method section with no alternative is
a method section that has not thought about its own choice.

## 6. Bibliography

- Author, A. (Year). *Title*. Publisher. DOI/URL.
